    ROM needed

    The emulator needs the original ROM: 48.rom (16,384 bytes) for the 48K, 128.rom (32,768 bytes) for the 128K. Drop it here, or press EJECT on the recorder to open it; it is remembered in this browser.

    1. Using the emulator

    This booklet is for anyone who has never used a ZX Spectrum, and for those who used one long ago and have forgotten how. Everything on the desk works: the computer, the television, the recorder and the gadgets around them.

    1. 1The computer. Choose it with the television's channel buttons: 48K, + or 128. Changing channel resets it.
    2. 2A tape. Press STOP/EJ on the recorder and pick a .tap or .tzx tape, a .z80 or .sna snapshot, or a ROM. You can also drop files anywhere on the page, or find a game in the software catalogue (F2, an empty place on the rack, or the empty cassette window). Every tape you load goes onto your cassette rack, so you can play it again later, even offline.
    3. 3The recorder. With AUTO LOAD on, the tape starts by itself. Otherwise type the load command and press PLAY.

    Loading a program by hand

    On the 48K and the Spectrum+, type

    LOAD ""
    (J, then SYMBOL SHIFT + P twice, then ENTER)

    and press PLAY. The border flashes while the program loads. On the 128, choose Tape Loader from the menu instead.

    Click the cassette to see what is on the tape; click a line to wind the tape to it.

    If something goes wrong

    Press the reset button on the side of the computer. The Multiface's red button stops a game so that you can save it and come back later.

    2. The desk

    Everything you need is on the desk. The screen is laid out thus:

    1. 1Multiface One. The red button freezes the game and shows its menu on the screen.
    2. 2Television. Double-click the picture for full screen.
    3. 3Channel buttons 48K, + and 128 choose the computer; VOLUME, COLOUR, ZOOM and POWER.
    4. 4Joystick. Click it to plug it in.
    5. 5This manual.
    6. 6ZX Spectrum. Type on your PC keyboard or click the keys.
    7. 7Reset button on the side of the computer.
    8. 8Datacorder. STOP/EJ opens a tape; click the cassette to see what is on it. With no tape in, the cassette window opens the catalogue.
    9. 9Cassette rack. Your tapes: click one to load it, an empty place to find a game, MY TAPES for the list, A to D for the other banks.

    Rest the mouse on any of them to see its name. On a phone they are arranged to fit the screen.

    4. Settings

    SPEED switchNORMAL: real tape speed, with the border stripes and the loading sound. FAST: the computer runs flat out while the tape plays. INSTANT: programs go straight into memory; games with a loader of their own (turbo, Speedlock and the like) run flat out with the picture still, so they load in seconds too.
    AUTO LOADPutting in a tape resets the computer and starts loading it (LOAD "" on the 48K and Spectrum+, Tape Loader on the 128). On the 128, a game made only for the 48K loads in 48 BASIC, as many of them need. A tape with a 48K and a 128K version starts at the one for your model.
    Tape parts and multi-loadSome tapes come in parts: two sides, a tape of levels, or the 48K and 128K versions. They go into the recorder as one tape, which stops between its parts. Click the cassette window to see the parts and their blocks; click one to wind the tape there. The tape starts by itself when a game listens for it, and stops at the end of a block when the game has what it wants, so a game that loads its levels one by one finds the next one waiting. If the tape runs out while a game still waits, the screen offers to rewind it, wind it to a part, or put in another tape. STOP keeps the tape still until you press PLAY.
    CounterShows how far the tape has played; the small button sets it to 000.
    MultifaceThe red button freezes the game and shows its menu on the screen: SAVE downloads a snapshot, LOAD opens one, TURBO runs as fast as possible, RESET restarts, RETURN carries on. Choose with the arrows and ENTER, or press S, L, T, X or R; BREAK returns to the game.
    Software catalogueType a title and press ENTER: ZXDB, the database of everything made for the Spectrum, and your own libraries are searched together. Move with the arrows (or CAPS SHIFT + 5 to 8), press ENTER to load, DELETE to go back. BREAK goes back to the first page, and on the first page it leaves the catalogue; deleting the whole title also brings back the first page. The line under the title bar says where you are. Titles in red cannot be loaded: their makers do not allow it, or they are newer than the archive. A green star marks a title you have: ENTER loads your file. You can also click a line. While a game downloads, the screen counts the seconds until the archive answers, then shows how much has arrived; BREAK cancels, and if the archive does not answer, ENTER tries again.
    Your librariesOn the catalogue's first page press DOWN (or click) to reach YOUR LIBRARIES, then UP and DOWN to move (the line above the keys always says what the arrows and letters do): F adds a folder of games (on a PC), Z a zip (on any device), O opens one file. Only the list of games is kept, so a library can be as big as you like; the games stay in your folder or zip and are read when you load one. Searches look in your libraries too, and your file is preferred to the archive's; games ZXDB does not know are shown in green. A TOSEC-style collection keeps many dumps of each game: they show as one line per game, without bad dumps (and without cracked, trained or hacked versions or demos when there is a clean one); with several good versions, ENTER lists them best first: verified [!], the original, then alternates [a]. A zip inside a library that holds no tape or snapshot (a disk image, say) is left out: each is checked the first time it would be shown, while the library is connected, and remembered. ENTER on a library lists its games: type to narrow the list. DELETE twice removes a library from the list (your files are not touched). Add a library once on each device. The browser forgets your files between visits, so a library then shows as not connected: R (Reconnect to my library) asks for it again; Chrome and Edge on a PC ask for permission, other browsers ask you to pick the zip or folder once more. Loading a game from a library that is not connected offers R, or the archive's copy if there is one.
    SavingAs in 1982: press REC on the recorder (REC and PLAY go down together; F5 on a PC), then let the program or game SAVE. It records onto a tape of your own on the rack: <game> saves, or My programs after a reset, for what you write in BASIC. If you forget REC, the screen asks. To load it back, LOAD "" (or the game's load option): when the recorder has nothing to load, the screen lets you pick a tape from the rack, or rewind this one. STOP ends the recording.
    My tapesThe rack keeps 32 tapes on this device, in four banks of 8 (A to D: the buttons under the rack), and they work offline: games from the catalogue, files you open and Multiface saves go onto it, in the bank on show while it has room. When all are full the screen asks which tape makes room. Click MY TAPES on the rack for the list: LEFT and RIGHT (or A to D) change the bank, ENTER loads, DELETE twice removes, O opens a file of your own, S saves a backup file of the whole rack and L loads one, on this or another device.
    JoystickClick it to plug it in; each click changes the kind, shown on its base: Kempston (most games), Sinclair (Interface 2: keys 6 to 0), Cursor (Protek/AGF: keys 5 to 8, fire 0), then unplugged. Choose the one the game's menu offers. The arrow keys and Left Alt work it. On a phone or tablet a stick appears under your left thumb and a FIRE button under your right. A real joystick or gamepad (USB or Bluetooth) works too: press one of its buttons so the browser sees it; the touch stick then goes away, and its d-pad or stick and main buttons work the kind chosen here.
    TelevisionZOOM (or a double-click on the picture) fills the screen; POWER pauses everything. Volume: drag the VOLUME knob, or roll the mouse wheel anywhere over the TV; click the word VOLUME to turn the sound off and on. Colour: drag the COLOUR knob or hold Shift with the wheel; click the word COLOUR for black and white or a green-screen monitor. The TV remembers its settings, so if you left it silent it stays silent.

    5. About

    An emulator of the Sinclair ZX Spectrum 48K (1982), Spectrum+ (1984) and Spectrum 128 (1986), written in plain JavaScript. It runs in the browser, with nothing to install.

    The Spectrum ROMs are copyright Amstrad, who allow them to be distributed for use with emulators.

    This is a fan project. It is not made, endorsed or supported by Sky, Sinclair Research or Amstrad; ZX Spectrum and Sinclair are trademarks of their owners. Search words typed into the catalogue go to ZXInfo; nothing else leaves your computer.

    The software catalogue searches ZXDB through the ZXInfo service and loads the games from the World of Spectrum archive kept at archive.org. Only titles whose makers allow it can be downloaded.

    Source code: github.com/akisbelibassakis/zx-spectrum

    A note from the author

    I was a teenager in the 1980s when a ZX Spectrum came into my life. Rubber keys, the family television, long hours typing in program listings from magazines, line by line, hunting for the one mistyped character, and long minutes of screeching tape, waiting to see whether this time the game would load. Those evenings taught me what a computer could be, and a little of who I would become.

    This emulator is my way of switching it on once more. I hope it brings back a few memories for you too.

    Akis Belibassakis
